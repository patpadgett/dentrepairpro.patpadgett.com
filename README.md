# dentrepairpro.patpadgett.com

Static one-page site for **Dent Repair Pro, LLC**, John Matthews' paintless dent repair (PDR)
business in Lenexa, Kansas. Hosted on GitHub Pages at https://dentrepairpro.patpadgett.com/.

The page is built around the tool PDR technicians actually use: a line board (or lamp) whose
reflection in the paint bends wherever the metal is low. The hero is a dark blue door under that
board, one ding pulling the reflected lines inward; beside it a bounded "Push the dent" card runs
a live WebGL panel the visitor flattens with a slider (or a tap), steering the lamp with the
pointer. Every other line on the site, from the logo mark to the process plates to the closing
field, is the same reflected line.

No build step. No dependencies at runtime. No analytics, no third-party requests. Headless
rendering (QA, plates, social card) needs Chromium with `--use-gl=swiftshader
--enable-unsafe-swiftshader --ignore-gpu-blocklist`; the scripts pass those flags. Serve the repo
root on 127.0.0.1:8765 first (`python3 -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1`).

## Deliberately NOT claimed (nothing here is invented)

- Years in business, number of repairs, certifications beyond the NAPDRT listing
- Mobile service, hours, service radius (the copy names Lenexa and says it sits on the Kansas side of the KC metro; it does not say where John travels)
- SMS or photo-based quoting (the worksheet suggests photos for the inspection, not as a quote channel)
- Prices (the FAQ explains how PDR is generally priced, never a number)
- Reviews, testimonials, before/after photos
- Email address (D&B hides it behind a paywall; none found elsewhere)
- Insurance claims handling
- That the paint is "never touched" (glue tabs and tap-downs do touch it); the claim is no filler, no repaint

All PDR explanation on the page (what qualifies, how the push works, aluminum, hail, body-line
dents, glue pulling) is general trade knowledge, written to be true of PDR as a method and not as
a promise about John's shop, with "usually / typically / often" where outcomes vary. Every image is
an illustration and is labeled as one on the page; no image depicts a real vehicle or a real repair.

## Design decisions (short version; DESIGN.md has the tokens)

- Direction: the PDR line board itself, chosen from a seven-candidate grounded list by the
  Impeccable concept roll (seed `0205b820`, assigned index 5, mode persuade). Recorded in
  `.impeccable/surfaces/index-html.md`.
- Colour strategy: committed. Gentian blue `#15309A` owns the hero, the comparison, and the
  closing; a cool white board `#F4F6FB` carries the reading sections. Lamp amber `#FFB454` is the
  only accent and appears only on interaction (slider thumb, focus rings, the rod tip, the dent
  circle in the photo guides) and as the lamp's reflection in the demo panel.
- Type: Big Shoulders Display 700–900 for display, Archivo for text. Both self-hosted.
- The hero is a material, not a diagram. Two finish reviews rejected procedural renders (stroked
  waves, then a full WebGL height field) as reading like "luminous strands on blue" rather than
  paint. The shipped hero is a composite: a locally generated photographic paint plate (Flux
  Schnell, prompt in `tools/prompt-t2i-c.txt`) carrying the material, with the board's reflection
  drawn over it in perspective, out of focus, bowing with the door's crown, wobbling with orange
  peel, framed, and dimmer away from its lamp; the ding is a monotonic radial lens that bends
  everything the paint reflects, not just the board. The demo and the process plates stay on the
  WebGL program because they are diagrams by nature and labeled as such.
- The dent in `panel.js` and `composite.html` is the same mapping, `hit = c + (hit − c) / (1 − k·g(r))`
  with a smooth bell `g`, monotonic in `r` so the reflection bunches toward the low spot without
  folding into loops.
- The worksheet never submits anything. It composes a phone script, keeps the suitability note
  separate from the first-person script, persists in `localStorage`, and offers copy / save-as-text.
  Without JavaScript it shows static call-preparation guidance and hides the copy/save buttons.
  There is no backend on GitHub Pages and no fake endpoint.
